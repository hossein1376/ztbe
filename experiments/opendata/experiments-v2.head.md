# Experiments v2: open-data evaluation (phase 2)

Date: 2026-10-21 (Asia/Tehran).
- Code: `experiments/opendata/`. Protocol and licences: `experiments/opendata/README.md`.
- Raw outputs: `experiments/opendata/results/`.
- Everything below §7 is generated by `aggregate.py`.

## 1. Setup in one paragraph

**Data and protocol.**
- Content-blind features: 36 keystroke features (34 shared across datasets) and 23 mouse features. Windows are N keystrokes or T seconds of active input.
- Enrolment always comes from chronologically earlier data than test, following eberz2017.
  - LASER is cross-session.
  - Tappy is cross-day, with test data starting at least 7 days after the enrolment week.
  - Aalto is the second half of the same session, so it is optimistic and labelled *intra*.
  - SapiMouse tests a 1-min session against a 3-min enrolment.
  - Balabit uses training sessions for enrolment and labelled test sessions for testing.
- Priors (z-score, quantile edges, LDA-16) and the attacker's dictionary come from **disjoint** users: 20,000 Aalto users, or half of the Tappy users (`_selfprior`).
- Zero-effort impostors are the test windows of up to 30 other evaluated users.

**Metrics.**
- Global threshold.
- Gini coefficient over per-user FRR and per-attacker FAR.

**Entropy.**
- I report a *guessing-curve* upper estimate of fuzzy min-entropy: −log2 of the fraction of targets accepted by the attacker's best single guess. Each guess is a population template, chosen greedily from 4000.
- This assumes the attacker can inject feature vectors and uses the verifier's own threshold. Larger dictionaries would lower it.

## 2. Per-feature variation (see table in §7)

**Most stable features.**
- On Aalto and LASER: median press-press latency, rollover fraction, zone hold means, and S→L / R→L latencies. ICC is 0.93–0.98 and the test-window within/between ratio is 0.22–0.40.
- Cross-session (LASER) the ratio rises to 0.35–0.42 for the same top features.
- Cross-day (Tappy, in-domain priors), pp_median and hold_mu_L/R stay usable (ratio 0.41–0.51).

**Rollover fraction on Tappy.** It does **not** survive there (ratio 1.19). A plausible but untested cause is the roughly 15.6 ms timer resolution.

**Least stable features.** All per-transition SD features and burstiness have ratio ≥ 0.9 cross-session (LASER) and cross-day (Tappy). On Aalto (intra-session) the per-transition SDs range from 0.75 to 1.22 and burstiness is 1.21.

**Normality.** Population ICCs are high, but features are non-Gaussian (phase-1 CMU result; belman2019). For that reason the quantizers use empirical equiprobable edges.

## 3. Quantizers: BER and Hamming-distance separation (table §7)

| Data | Quantizer | Genuine / impostor BER | DoF | EER |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aalto n200 | Gray m=1 | 0.18 / 0.50 | 5 | 18.7% |
| Aalto n200 | LSSC S=8 | 0.14 / 0.37 | 12 | 7.5% |
| Aalto n200 | Population-mask LSSC S=8 (top-16 by Fisher ratio) | 0.09 / 0.38 | 7 | 7.4% |
| Aalto n200 | LDA-16 + LSSC S=8 (112 bits) | 0.16 / 0.37 | 40 | 5.7% |
| LASER, cross-session | Population-mask LSSC S=8 | 0.12–0.16 / 0.33 | 13–17 | 8.6–16.5% |
| Aalto | DROBA L=64 | — | — | 9.7–16.0% |
| Aalto | 1-bit DROBA-style user mask | 0.07–0.13 genuine | 3–4 | 15–20% |

Notes on the table:
- **LSSC impostor BER.** LSSC is a thermometer code, so impostor BER is about 0.37 by construction, not 0.5. Compare genuine against impostor BER, or use EER, rather than reading BER alone.
- **Population-mask LSSC** is the best binary variant cross-session on LASER.
- **DROBA** (user-specific allocation) is worse than population-level choices on every dataset.
- **1-bit DROBA-style user mask.** Its genuine BER is low (0.07–0.13), but only because it keeps each user's 16 most reliable bits. That gives DoF 3–4 and EER 15–20%.

**Cross-day (Tappy) BER.**
- With in-domain priors, LSSC S=8 is at 20.9% EER (n200).
- With Aalto priors the quantizers collapse to impostor BER about 0.14 (EER 24.7%), because of domain shift in the quantile edges.
- Binary templates therefore need **in-domain priors**.

**Phase-1 assumption.** The genuine BER of 0.15–0.25 assumed in spec v1 is reached only by the population-masked and LSSC codes. Gray m=1/2 sits at 0.18–0.34.

| Tappy, self priors (cross-day) | n400 | 6.8% | 8.9% | 8.4% |

LDA trained on only about 60 in-domain Tappy users is unstable: its EER ranges from 8.9% to 16.4% while SM ranges from 6.8% to 11.7%.

**FRR at FAR=1%** is 20–60% across all keystroke runs. The Gini of per-user FRR is 0.5–0.9, so errors concentrate on a minority of users (eberz2017).

**Mouse:**
- SapiMouse: EER 21–28%.
- Balabit: EER 28–34%. Its FRR@FAR1% exceeds 85%.

The mouse features are basic and not tuned. These numbers are therefore a floor for the feature set, not for the modality.

**Intra-session vs cross-session on the same LASER users.**
- LASER t60: 6.1% EER intra-session versus 12.1% cross-session.
- LASER n100: 7.9% intra-session versus 16.4% cross-session.

The roughly 2× degradation is the Eberz effect. The Aalto numbers above are intra-session and therefore optimistic.

## 5. Window length (tables §7)

**Keystroke EER by window length (scaled-Manhattan):**

| Data | Count-based windows (EER) | Time-based windows (EER) |
|---|---|---|
| Aalto | n50 14.5%, n100 10.9%, n200 8.6% | t30 13.1%, t60 9.7% |
| LASER, cross-session | n50 21.0%, n100 16.4%, n200 14.5% | t30 16.0%, t60 12.1%, t120 9.5% |

- **Tappy, cross-day, Aalto priors (n100 / n200 / n400; t60 / t120 / t300):** EER 9.2 / 7.9 / 7.0% and 10.0 / 8.4 / 6.6%. FRR@FAR1% 50.0 / 41.5 / 34.3% and 54.4 / 33.3 / 29.6%.
- **Tappy, self priors:** EER 10.9 / 9.4 / 6.8% and 11.7 / 9.8 / 7.0%.
- **5-minute windows** (Tappy t300) therefore reach EER of about 7%, but FRR@FAR1% is still about 30%.
- **Aalto, 5-minute windows:** not possible. Sessions have only about 2–3 minutes of active typing.
- **Decision fusion over k consecutive windows** (§7): EER drops only slowly with k, for example Tappy t60 goes from 10.3% to 6.4% at k=16. That is because errors are per-user systematic: the Gini of per-user FRR rises with k.

## 6. Entropy / guessing (table §7)

**Best single population guess, at the verifier's own threshold.** For each run, the share of targets broken by one guess:

| Data | At FAR=1% | At EER |
|---|---|---|
| Aalto | 6.4–22.4% | 25–69% |
| LASER | 0–40% | 5–75% |
| Tappy, self priors | 3.5–20.6% | 15–67% |
| SapiMouse | 0–25% | 38–92% |

Balabit is omitted: it has only 10 users and its priors come from the same users, so its guessing numbers (0–100%) carry no information.

LASER has 20 users, so its values move in steps of 0.05.

**Upper-estimate fuzzy min-entropy:**
- **2–4 bits** at FAR=1% (sometimes up to about 6 bits for Gray-1 on tiny mouse sets).
- **≈ 0.5–2 bits** at EER.

**Guesses needed for 50% success at FAR=1%** on Aalto: 4–92 for unprotected and LSSC variants. Gray m=1 does not reach 50% within its dictionary (P100 ≈ 0.4–0.5).

**Sample-then-lock on Aalto n200, LDA-LSSC 112 bits, ℓ=1500:**

| k | FRR | FAR | Targets with a locker opened by one population-mode guess |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | 7.6% | 7.6% | 52% |
| 32 | 30.5% | 1.1% | 15% |

**Caveat: Tappy with Aalto priors.** The Aalto dictionary does not transfer to Tappy, which produces "∞ bits". That is a domain-shift artefact, **not** security. Use the `_selfprior` rows.

**Conclusion.** The offline guessing work against any keyless construction is a handful of guesses, in line with phase-1 R2/R3.
